Research Interests

My research sits at the intersection of artificial intelligence and real-world problem solving. I'm particularly interested in making large language models more capable, efficient, and reliable for scientific and practical applications.

LLM Agents Efficient ML Systems Planning & Compositional Reasoning Alignment & Domain Adaptation Evaluation Multimodal Learning Reinforcement Learning Retrieval Augmented Generation

Current Focus

Teaching LLMs to Reason

How can we teach language models to break down complex, multi-step problems? I'm developing benchmarks and training techniques to evaluate and improve compositional reasoning in scientific domains like polymer design.

Efficient Adaptation

Exploring how models can be efficiently adapted to specialized domains without catastrophic forgetting. This includes work on alignment techniques (SFT, RLHF) and their long-term effects on model capabilities.

Multimodal Intelligence

Working on vision-language models with a focus on efficiency. How can we make these large models faster and more practical through techniques like adaptive token pruning?

Narrative Understanding

Analyzing how information and narratives evolve across different media sources and languages. This work combines NLP with social science to understand information dynamics at scale.

Research Philosophy

I believe the most impactful AI research bridges theory and practice. While I'm fascinated by fundamental questions about how models learn and reason, I'm equally committed to building systems that solve real problems.

Good research should be rigorous but accessible, ambitious but grounded. I strive to create work that advances our understanding while remaining useful to practitioners and scientists in other domains.

Beyond publishing papers, I'm passionate about open science—sharing code, datasets, and insights that help the broader community move forward together.
